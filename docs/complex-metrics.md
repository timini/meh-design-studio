# Complex linear metrics

`meh_studio.metrics` starts B05 with explicit RMS calculations under the existing `exp(-i omega t)` convention. It is a mathematical library, not a completed scoring pipeline. Version `linear-rms-v1` accompanies serialised pressure-level records. No conversion from Boundary Lab's native amplitude convention is inferred; native responses must not be fed into absolute SPL or power calculations until that convention is independently established.

`sum_voltage_basis` combines complex transfer responses shaped `(frequency, source, target)` using RMS input voltages shaped `(frequency, source)`. Basis responses must be normalised per RMS volt. It matches unique source identities and reorders the voltage columns. All frequency samples, loads and inactive-source electrical terminations must be identical across the basis and requested combination. Frequency coordinates and provenance are the caller's responsibility in this initial array API; it does not align, interpolate or qualify them. A zero voltage still represents a connected voltage source. Sum pressure before converting to decibels: summing SPL values or magnitudes would lose cancellation.

`gain_delay_voltages` generates signed gains and delays. With the negative-time phasor convention, a positive delay has factor `exp(+i omega delay)`, since its real time signal is `cos(omega*(t-delay))`. This function implements no crossover filter or amplifier limits.

`electrical_power_rms` returns each channel's signed real power and its net sum: `Re(V*conj(I))`, with current positive into the system. Negative channel power is retained, allowing regeneration through a coupled system. It never substitutes apparent power or clips a failing passivity result. Efficiency remains unavailable without appropriately established radiated power and input conditions.

`pressure_levels_rms` returns finite dB values referenced to 20 microPa at the supplied observation position. Exact zero becomes `value: null` with reason `zero_pressure`; there is no hidden dB floor. The caller must treat that null as an explicit cancellation requiring its declared objective policy, never silently omit it from optimisation. Missing, nonfinite or shape-invalid arrays are rejected. No distance correction, gain normalisation or acoustic validity claim is added.

`SphereQuadrature` specifies full-sphere directions and solid-angle weights. Samples follow polar-major, azimuth-minor ordering on one common radius. It integrates Gauss–Legendre nodes in `z=cos(theta)` and uniform periodic azimuth without duplicating its endpoint. The weights sum to `4*pi`. [NumPy documents the polynomial exactness and tested degree range of its Gauss–Legendre implementation](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.polynomial.legendre.leggauss.html). The API returns mean squared pressure in Pa², not radiated acoustic power or a qualified directivity index. Partial polar cuts cannot substitute for the sphere. The caller must actually sample the requested directions; array dimensions alone cannot prove that it did so. Angular refinement remains necessary for narrow lobes and nulls.

Checks include direct time-domain delay and power calculations, coherent addition/cancellation, source identity reordering, known pressure references, and analytic full-sphere integrals for monopoles, rotated dipoles and a quadrupole. A coupled circuit basis is synthesised and compared with a direct combined excitation, including real-power conservation. These verify the calculations, not acoustic solver accuracy or physical loudspeaker performance.

Still required: a hashed evaluation-to-metrics adapter with explicit amplitude calibration, frequency/coordinate provenance, response-error weighting, directivity/beamwidth validity, uncertainty, level and excursion constraints, efficiency, and optimiser policies for unknown/undefined values. This increment cannot rank a speaker as hi-fi or manufacture a qualified result.
